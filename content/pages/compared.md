The Vanik Matrimonial Register is a small, not-for-profit register for Gujarati Hindu and Jain families, with a flat £10 a year fee and volunteer ID checks on every member. Shaadi.com and Jeevansathi are large commercial matrimonial sites with far bigger memberships and mobile apps, while dating apps are built mainly around casual swiping and chat. Which suits you depends on what you value most.

## The short version

- Choose the **Vanik Matrimonial Register** if you want a community-specific, verified, low-pressure register run by volunteers, at a fixed low price.
- Choose a **large matrimonial site** if you want the widest possible choice across communities and countries, and are happy with paid tiers to unlock features.
- Choose a **dating app** if you want to meet lots of people quickly and are comfortable with a more casual, chat-first approach.

Many people use more than one. There is nothing wrong with that.

## Not-for-profit vs commercial

The Vanik Matrimonial Register is run by Vanik Council (UK), a registered charity in England and Wales (number 1137083), and has been running for over 40 years. It is not-for-profit, shows no adverts and never sells data.

Shaadi.com and Jeevansathi are commercial businesses. That is not a criticism: it pays for large teams, marketing and polished apps. It does mean their business depends on members upgrading, while the register has no reason to push you to spend more or stay longer.

## Cost

The register costs £10 a year, payable by card. Every member has the same features, and there are no upgrades.

At the time of writing (November 2026), Shaadi.com's own website says free members can create a profile, browse profiles and send interests, while premium membership is needed for things like starting and replying to messages, advanced filters and seeing contact details of interested members. Jeevansathi's website offers free registration, with paid membership needed to message and chat with unlimited users. Both sites set their own prices, which can vary, so check them directly.

## Verification

On the register, a Vanik Council volunteer checks every application, including the photo page of a passport or a driving licence, usually within 2 working days. Nobody can browse or be seen until they are approved. The ID image is deleted after approval.

At the time of writing (November 2026), Shaadi.com's help pages describe a selfie verification, where a selfie is compared with your profile photo and a blue tick is added if it matches. Jeevansathi's website mentions screening and phone number verification. Dating apps vary widely, and many rely mainly on photo or phone checks.

## Community focus

Large sites serve many communities across the world, which brings huge choice. On the register, everyone comes from a Gujarati Hindu or Jain family, so shared background, food and festivals are a starting point. Profiles show diet, religion and family origin, and you can filter by diet and religion. Read more for [Jain families](/jain-matrimonial-uk) and [Gujarati Hindu families](/gujarati-hindu-matrimonial-uk).

## Endless chatting vs introductions

Dating apps and many matrimonial sites are built around messaging, which can mean long chats that never lead anywhere. The register works differently. You browse, save and then request an introduction, and both of you receive each other's details. You can have up to three introductions at a time, with fair weekly and monthly limits, so each one is a considered choice. Our guide on [a register or a dating app](/guides/register-or-dating-app) explores this further.

## Privacy

On the register, names, photos and contact details are never displayed publicly. Browsing cards show no names or photos, and nobody is told who looked at their profile. Photos and contact details are shared only when an introduction is made. See our [safety page](/safety).

Large sites and apps have their own privacy settings, and some give you more options to adjust than the register does. It is worth comparing these directly on each service.

## What do the bigger services do better?

It is only fair to be clear about this:

- **Far larger membership.** The big sites have many more members, in many more places. The register is small, and in some areas and age groups you will see fewer people.
- **Mobile apps.** Shaadi.com and Jeevansathi have dedicated apps. The register works through your phone's web browser, but has no app.
- **In-app messaging and calls.** If you like to chat before sharing a phone number, the bigger services offer that. The register shares mobile numbers on introduction instead.
- **More search options.** Large sites offer more detailed filters. The register keeps things simple: age, height, diet and religion.

## So which is right for me?

If you are from a Gujarati Hindu or Jain family and want a verified, respectful, community-run way to meet people who are serious about marriage, the register is a good place to start, and at £10 a year it sits easily alongside anything else you use.
