Every member of the Vanik Matrimonial Register is checked by a volunteer, including proof of identity, before their profile appears, and your name, photos and contact details are never displayed publicly. This page explains exactly what other members can and cannot see, and how to stay safe when you meet someone. It is written with women in mind, but it applies to everyone.

## How are members verified?

Every application is reviewed by a Vanik Council volunteer, usually within 2 working days. Applicants upload the photo page of their passport or their driving licence, and the volunteer checks it against the details given. The ID image is used only for this check and is deleted after approval.

Volunteers also check photos and profile details. Group photos and unclear pictures are not accepted. Only approved members can browse, so everyone you see has been through the same check. There are no bots and no fake accounts.

## What can other members see about me?

It helps to know exactly what is shared, and when.

**While browsing, other approved members see:** your age, job title, where you live, religion, diet, height, nationality and any settlement plans you added. When they open your profile they can also read your education, family origin and hobbies.

**While browsing, they do not see:** your name, your photos or any contact details. Nobody is told that you looked at their profile, and nobody can see that someone looked at yours.

**After an introduction:** when a member requests you, or you request them, you both receive each other's name, photos, full profile and mobile number (plus a LinkedIn link if one was added). You get an email so you know who to expect to hear from. Your home address is not shared with other members.

**The public:** nothing that identifies you is displayed publicly. The free [profile preview](/demo) shows visitors anonymised cards with basic details such as age, job and where someone lives, but never names, photos, contact details or full profiles.

## Who can contact me, and how often?

Only approved, verified members can request an introduction. Each member can have up to three introductions at a time, with fair weekly and monthly limits, so nobody can contact large numbers of people at once.

The register does not have a chat or messaging inbox. Introductions happen once, and then the two of you decide whether to talk.

If you need a break, you can pause your profile at any time. Pausing hides you from browse and saved lists straight away, and you can unpause when you are ready.

## What happens if someone behaves badly?

Every member agrees to use the register respectfully and to give accurate information. Misuse of contact details, harassment or misrepresentation can lead to suspension or removal from the register.

After each introduction you are asked for short feedback. It goes only to the volunteer team, is never shown to the other member, and is used to flag any concerns. If something worries you at any time, email matrimonial@vanikcouncil.uk and a volunteer will look into it.

## How is my data protected?

The register is run by Vanik Council, a registered charity, under UK data protection law. The service is not-for-profit, shows no adverts and never sells your data. Card payments are handled by a specialist payment provider, so your card details are not stored by the register. Read the full [privacy policy](/privacy) for details.

## Tips for meeting someone safely

These are sensible steps for anyone meeting a new person, whoever introduced you.

- **Talk first.** A phone or video call before meeting helps you get a feel for someone.
- **Meet in a public place.** Choose a busy café or restaurant for the first few meetings, and arrange your own travel there and back.
- **Tell someone.** Let a friend or family member know where you are going, who you are meeting and when you expect to be home.
- **Keep personal details back.** There is no need to share your home address, workplace details or financial information early on.
- **Never send money.** No genuine match will ask you for money. If anyone does, stop contact and tell us.
- **Trust your instincts.** If something feels wrong, you can end the conversation at any point. You do not owe anyone an explanation.
- **Involve family if you wish.** Many people find it reassuring to let parents know about an introduction early on.
