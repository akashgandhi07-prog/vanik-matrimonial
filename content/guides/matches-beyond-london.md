If you live outside the larger Gujarati areas, the key is to widen your search beyond your own town, be open early about where you could live, and use a UK-wide service so distance does not decide who you can meet. Many couples start long-distance and agree where to settle once they know each other well.

## Why is it harder outside the big Gujarati areas?

North-West London and Leicester have large Gujarati communities, with mandirs, derasars, samaj events and dense family networks. If you grew up there, introductions can come through friends, cousins and community life without much effort.

If you live in Cardiff, Newcastle, Bristol, Glasgow, Norwich or a smaller town, or you moved away for university or work, those networks may be thinner. You might be the only Gujarati family in your street, or one of a handful at your workplace. That does not mean there is nobody for you. It means you need a slightly wider net.

## How do you widen your search?

- **Use a UK-wide register.** The Vanik Matrimonial Register is mainly UK-based and open to adults from all Gujarati Hindu and Jain families, Vanik or not, wherever they live. Members abroad are welcome too.
- **Think about where you would realistically live.** Rather than only searching your own city, decide which places you could imagine living in, and be open to people there.
- **Stay connected to community life.** Regional samaj events, festival celebrations and family weddings are still good places to meet people and to hear about others who are looking.
- **Go to events further afield occasionally.** Vanik Council runs [speed dating evenings](/guides/speed-dating-night) twice a year. Travelling for an evening can be worth it.
- **Tell trusted family and friends you are open to introductions.** Networks reach further than you might expect.

## What should your profile say about location?

Be clear and specific. The register profile includes where you live and an optional section for future settlement plans. Useful examples:

- "Live in Manchester, happy to relocate within the UK."
- "Based in Bristol for work, would like to move back towards Leicester in a few years."
- "Settled in Glasgow and would love my partner to join me here."
- "Open to living anywhere in the UK. Not looking to move abroad."

Clarity saves time for everyone. If you are flexible, say so, because that alone can open up many more introductions. Our guide to [writing your profile](/guides/writing-your-profile) has more tips.

## How do you manage the long-distance early stages?

Starting long-distance is common and can work well, especially as early conversations often happen by phone anyway.

- **Start with calls.** A phone or video call or two before meeting helps you both decide whether a journey is worth it.
- **Meet somewhere convenient.** Halfway between your cities, or near a main train station, makes a first meeting easier. A café near the station means neither person has a long journey after a short meeting.
- **Share the travel.** Take turns travelling so one person is not always making the effort.
- **Plan longer second meetings.** If the first one goes well, a half-day together, perhaps a walk and lunch, gives you more time to talk.
- **Keep up regular contact.** Agree a rhythm of calls that suits you both, without it feeling like a chore.
- **Stay safe.** Book your own travel and accommodation, meet in public, and tell someone at home where you are. Our [staying safe](/guides/staying-safe) guide covers this in more detail.

## How do you decide where to live?

At some point, one or both of you may need to move. This deserves honest conversation, ideally before families become formally involved. Think about:

- **Work.** Whose job is more location-dependent? Could either of you work remotely, or transfer?
- **Family.** Are there parents who need support nearby? How important is it to each of you to live close to your ghar and family?
- **Community.** Would you like to live near a mandir, derasar or Gujarati community, or does that matter less?
- **Cost of living and housing.** Moving from a smaller city to London, or the other way round, can change your finances considerably.
- **Children.** Where would you like to bring up a family, and near whom?
- **Fairness.** If one person moves now, could the other move later if circumstances change?

Our guide to [questions to ask before saying yes](/guides/questions-to-ask-before-marriage) includes more on living arrangements.

## What about relocating for marriage?

Moving to a new city for a partner can be exciting and also lonely at first. A few things help:

- **Visit together beforehand.** Spend time in the new place, not just at your partner's home.
- **Keep your own life going.** Look for work, friends, hobbies and community groups of your own there.
- **Stay close to your family.** Regular calls and visits home ease the transition.
- **Be patient with yourself.** Settling in takes time, even when you are happy.

The partner who stays put has a role too: introducing their partner to friends, sharing the effort of making the new place feel like home, and being understanding when there are homesick days.

## What if your partner lives abroad?

The register welcomes members abroad, and some people are open to an international match. If that is you, talk early and practically about visas, careers, where you would settle, and how often you could see each other before marriage. These conversations are much easier at the start than later.
