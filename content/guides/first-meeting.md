A first meeting after an introduction works well when it is short, public and relaxed: a coffee or a walk for an hour or so, with easy conversation about everyday life rather than a full interview about marriage. The only goal is to find out whether you would both like to meet again.

## How do you arrange the first meeting?

Once you have each other's contact details, a short message or phone call is a good start. Something simple works:

"Hi, it's Priya from the Vanik Matrimonial Register. Thanks for getting in touch. Would you like to have a quick call this week, and maybe meet for a coffee after that?"

A phone or video call before meeting in person helps both of you feel more comfortable. Ten or fifteen minutes is enough to check you get on and agree a plan.

When suggesting a time and place, offer a couple of options and let the other person choose. Daytime or early evening on a weekend tends to suit most people.

## Where should you meet?

Choose somewhere public, easy to reach and quiet enough to talk:

- A café or tea room in a town centre.
- A walk in a busy park, followed by a hot drink.
- A museum or gallery café.
- A casual restaurant for lunch, if you both prefer a meal.

Avoid places that are very loud, very expensive, or at either person's home. If you live in different cities, meeting halfway or near a station often works well. Our guide on [finding a match beyond London](/guides/matches-beyond-london) has ideas for long-distance first meetings.

If either of you keeps a vegetarian or Jain diet, pick somewhere with good options so nobody feels awkward ordering.

## Should parents come along?

Families differ. In some, it is traditional for parents to meet early, sometimes even before the couple. Many people now prefer to meet one-to-one first and involve families once they both feel positive.

Talk about this openly beforehand so there are no surprises. If parents do come, it is kind to give the two of you some time to talk alone. For more on how parents can help without adding pressure, see our [guide for parents](/guides/parents-guide).

## What should you talk about?

Start light. You do not need to cover marriage, children and finances in the first hour. Good first-meeting topics include:

- Work and what you enjoy about it, or don't.
- Where you grew up and where your family is from.
- How you spend weekends and holidays.
- Food, travel, books, films, sport or anything in their profile that caught your eye.
- Family, in a relaxed way: siblings, cousins, family traditions you love.
- What made each of you decide to look for a partner now.

Ask open questions and follow up on the answers. If they mention a recent trip, ask what surprised them about it. Listening well is more attractive than having perfect stories ready.

It is fine to acknowledge the slightly unusual setup. "I've not done this before, have you?" can break the ice nicely.

## What should you avoid on a first meeting?

- **Interviewing.** A list of questions about salary, property and how many children someone wants can feel like a job assessment.
- **Talking about exes in detail.** A brief, honest mention if it comes up is fine. A long story is not.
- **Checking your phone.** Put it away unless you are expecting something genuinely important.
- **Over-sharing family pressure.** "My mum wants me married by next year" puts a lot on someone you have just met.
- **Judging too fast.** Nerves make people quieter or chattier than usual. One slightly awkward hour is not the full picture.

## Who pays?

There is no fixed rule. Offering to split the bill, or taking turns paying on different meetings, is common and avoids awkwardness. If the other person insists on paying, accept graciously and offer to get the next one.

## How do you know if it went well?

Ask yourself a few simple questions on the way home:

- Did I feel comfortable and respected?
- Was the conversation easy for at least part of the time?
- Am I curious to know more about them?

If the answer is yes, that is enough reason to meet again. Strong chemistry on day one is lovely, but many good marriages start with a quiet sense of ease that grows over time.

## How do you follow up afterwards?

Send a short message within a day or so. If you would like to meet again, say so warmly: "I really enjoyed chatting today. Would you like to meet again, maybe for a walk next weekend?"

If you do not feel it is right, it is kind to say so clearly rather than going quiet:

"Thank you for meeting me today, I enjoyed our conversation. I don't think we're the right match, but I wish you every happiness in your search."

That small courtesy matters, especially in a close-knit samaj where families may know each other.

## When should the bigger questions come in?

Once you are both keen to keep meeting, start bringing in the topics that shape married life: values, family, where you will live, money, faith and children. Our guide to [questions to ask before saying yes](/guides/questions-to-ask-before-marriage) has a full list and tips on raising them gently.

## Staying safe

Meet in public, tell a friend or family member where you are going, arrange your own transport, and keep personal details like your home address private until you know someone well. Trust your instincts. If anything makes you uncomfortable, you can leave. Read more in our guide on [staying safe when meeting someone new](/guides/staying-safe).


## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a first meeting after an introduction last?

About an hour is ideal. It is long enough to get a feel for each other and short enough that neither of you feels stuck if it isn't working. If it is going well, it is better to finish while you both still have things to say and arrange a second meeting.

### What should I wear to a first matrimonial meeting?

Smart casual, in something you feel comfortable and like yourself in. Think of what you might wear to a nice lunch with friends rather than a family function. If you are meeting outdoors, dress for the weather and for walking.

### Should families meet before the couple meets?

It depends on the family. Some still prefer parents to meet early, but many couples now meet one-to-one first and involve families once they both feel positive. Agree on it openly beforehand so nobody is surprised on the day.

### How soon should I message after a first meeting?

Within a day or so. A short, warm message thanking them and saying whether you would like to meet again is enough. If you don't feel it is right, a kind, clear message is better than going quiet.

### How do I politely say no after a first meeting?

Thank them, say clearly that you don't think you are the right match, and wish them well. For example: "Thank you for meeting me, I enjoyed our conversation. I don't think we're the right match, but I wish you every happiness." You don't need to give a detailed reason.

### What if I am nervous about meeting someone?

Almost everyone is. Have a short phone or video call first, choose a place you know, and plan one or two easy questions. Saying "I'm a bit nervous, I've not done this before" often relaxes you both.
