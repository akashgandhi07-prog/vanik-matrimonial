## What happens at a typical speed dating evening?

Formats vary from organiser to organiser, so treat this as a general picture rather than a description of any particular event. A common pattern looks like this:

1. **Arrival and check-in.** Guests arrive, sign in and are usually given a name badge and a scorecard or a way to note who they would like to see again.
2. **A short welcome.** The host explains how the evening works and how long each conversation lasts.
3. **Timed conversations.** You sit opposite one person and chat for a few minutes. When the time is up, a signal sounds and one group moves on to the next table.
4. **A break.** Many evenings include a short pause for drinks or snacks, which is also a chance to chat more informally.
5. **Choosing.** Privately, you note who you would be happy to meet again.
6. **Matches afterwards.** Organisers typically share contact details only where both people have said yes, often by email after the event.

The mutual-match approach is what makes speed dating feel low-pressure. Nobody finds out if you did not tick their name.

## Who is speed dating suited to?

Speed dating can suit you if:

- You find it easier to get a sense of someone face to face than from a profile.
- You would like to meet several people in one evening.
- You enjoy a bit of energy and a sociable atmosphere.
- You are new to looking for a partner and want a gentle way to start.

It can feel less natural if you are very shy in groups, though many people find the structure actually helps. You always know what is coming next, and every conversation has a clear end.

## How should you prepare?

- **Read the event details carefully.** Check the time, venue, age range and any dress guidance in the announcement.
- **Plan your journey.** Arrive a little early so you are not flustered.
- **Eat something beforehand** if the event does not mention food.
- **Wear something comfortable that you feel good in.** Smart casual is usually a safe choice.
- **Bring a friend if allowed.** Going with a friend who is also single can make arriving easier.
- **Charge your phone.** You may want it for your journey home or to note thoughts afterwards.

## What should you talk about in a few minutes?

Short conversations go fast. You will not cover everything, and that is fine. The aim is to find out whether you would enjoy a longer chat.

Good openers:

- "What brought you here tonight?"
- "What do you do when you're not working?"
- "Where's your family from originally?"
- "What's the last thing you did that you really enjoyed?"
- "Are you a big foodie? What would you cook if you were having friends over?"

Listen and follow up. One genuine follow-up question is worth more than five rapid ones. If there is a pause, smile. Everyone is in the same boat.

Avoid heavy topics such as salary, past relationships or detailed marriage plans. There will be time for those if you meet again.

## How do you decide who to choose?

Go with your instinct. Ask yourself: "Would I like to have a coffee with this person and talk for longer?" That is all a yes means at this stage. It is not a commitment.

Some people only tick one or two names, others tick several. Both are fine. Be honest, as there is no benefit to saying yes to someone you are not interested in.

## What happens after a match?

If you match with someone, send a friendly message within a few days and suggest a coffee or a phone call. Our guide to your [first meeting](/guides/first-meeting) covers where to meet, what to talk about and how to follow up.

If you do not match with anyone, please do not be discouraged. A few minutes is not much time, and nerves affect everyone. Many people find the evening useful practice and come away more confident.

## Staying safe

A speed dating evening is a hosted, organised setting, which helps. For any follow-up meeting, the usual sensible steps apply: meet in a public place, tell someone your plans, arrange your own transport and keep personal details private until you know someone well.
