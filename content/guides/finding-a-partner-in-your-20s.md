Looking for a life partner in your 20s does not have to mean rushing. You can start gently, at your own pace, by meeting people who share your values and are also thinking about marriage, while still focusing on your studies, career and friendships. Starting early simply gives you more time to get to know people properly.

## Is it too early to be looking?

Not if you feel ready to start thinking about it. There is no correct age. Some people know in their early twenties that they would like to marry within a few years. Others want to finish training, settle into a job or move out first. Both are completely normal.

What matters is your own readiness, not someone else's timetable. A useful question is: "If I met someone lovely next month, would I be glad?" If the answer is yes, it is a good time to begin looking, even if you are not in a hurry.

The Vanik Matrimonial Register is open to adults from 18, so you can join whenever it feels right for you, not when someone else decides it should.

## Can you look for marriage without feeling pressured?

Yes, and it helps to set your own terms at the start. Decide privately:

- **How much time you want to give it.** Perhaps one or two introductions a month, rather than every evening.
- **What you are and are not ready for.** Maybe you want to meet people now but are thinking of marriage in two or three years. Saying so honestly is fine.
- **Who you tell.** You might involve your parents from day one, or prefer to wait until you have met someone you like.

Looking does not commit you to anything. Meeting someone for coffee is just that.

## How do you handle family expectations?

Many people in their twenties feel caught between wanting their family's support and wanting to make their own choices. A few things can help:

- **Have one calm conversation rather than many tense ones.** Tell your parents where you are: "I'd like to meet someone, but I'm not in a rush. I'll tell you if I meet someone I like."
- **Give them a role.** Parents often relax when they feel included. Ask them to help with photos, or to look at your profile with you.
- **Set gentle boundaries.** It is fine to ask relatives not to share your details without asking you first.
- **Remember they mean well.** Most pressure comes from love and worry, even when it does not feel like it.

If it would help, share our [guide for parents](/guides/parents-guide) with them. It encourages support without steering.

## What about career and studies?

Your twenties are often busy with university, training, first jobs and perhaps a move to a new city. Looking for a partner can fit around that.

Be honest on your profile about where you are. "Trainee accountant, qualifying next year" or "Junior doctor, currently in Birmingham" helps people understand your life. Someone who is right for you will respect your ambitions, and you should look for someone whose plans you can support too.

It is also worth thinking about location. If your job may take you to another city, or you hope to move back near family, the future settlement plans section on your profile is the place to say so. Our guide on [finding a match beyond London](/guides/matches-beyond-london) has more on this.

## How do you write a profile that feels like you?

Keep it natural. Talk about what you actually do: the five-a-side team, the book club, the cooking experiments, the volunteering at the mandir. Use clear, recent photos. Be honest about diet, faith and plans.

You do not need a polished life story at 24. Curiosity, warmth and a sense of humour come across well. Our guide on [writing your profile](/guides/writing-your-profile) has practical tips.

## What if you meet someone and things move quickly?

Enjoy it, and still take your time with the big questions. Talk about values, family, money, faith and children before you make commitments. Our guide to [questions to ask before saying yes](/guides/questions-to-ask-before-marriage) is a good place to start.

If you are not ready, say so kindly. A good partner will understand that you want to be sure.

## What if nothing happens straight away?

That is normal too. Finding the right person can take time, and your twenties give you plenty of it. Keep living a full life: friends, hobbies, work, family. Those are also the things that make you an interesting person to meet.

You might also enjoy community events. Vanik Council runs [speed dating evenings](/guides/speed-dating-night) twice a year, which can be a relaxed way to meet a few people in one night.
