Staying safe means taking the same sensible care with every new person, however they were introduced: verify who they are, keep personal details private at first, meet in public, and tell someone where you are going. On the Vanik Matrimonial Register, a volunteer checks every application, including proof of identity, before anyone can browse, but your own good habits still matter.

## How does the Vanik Matrimonial Register verify members?

Every application is checked by a Vanik Council volunteer, and that includes proof of identity. This usually happens within 2 working days. Only once an application has been approved can that person browse profiles and request introductions.

A few other things help keep the register private and safe:

- **Contact details stay private.** They are never displayed publicly. Phone numbers and emails are shared only inside the member dashboard, and only when someone requests them.
- **Introductions are limited.** Members can have up to three introductions at a time, with fair weekly and monthly limits. This keeps things personal and discourages anyone from contacting large numbers of people.
- **The person looking to marry holds the account.** Parents are welcome to help, but each member creates their own account, so the identity checked is the identity of the person you will speak to.
- **No adverts, data never sold.** The register is run on a not-for-profit basis by volunteers of Vanik Council (UK), registered charity 1137083.

You can read more on our [safety page](/safety).

## Does verification mean you can skip your own checks?

No. Verification confirms that a person is who they say they are. It cannot tell you whether someone is right for you, or guarantee how they will behave. Treat a verified profile as a good starting point, then take the same care you would with anyone new.

## How should you handle early messages and calls?

- **Take your time.** There is no need to share everything at once. Let trust build over several conversations.
- **Keep sensitive details private at first.** Your home address, workplace details, financial information and family addresses can wait until you know someone well.
- **Have a phone or video call before meeting.** A short video call is a simple way to confirm the person matches their photos and to see whether you feel comfortable.
- **Notice how they respond to boundaries.** If you say you would rather not share something yet, a respectful person will accept that easily.
- **Be careful with photos.** Think before sending personal photos to someone you have not met.

## What are the warning signs?

Most people you meet will be genuine and kind. Still, be cautious if someone:

- Asks for money, gifts, bank details or help with a "temporary" financial problem, however reasonable the story sounds.
- Pushes to move very fast, such as talking about marriage dates or moving in after a couple of conversations.
- Avoids video calls or always has a reason not to meet.
- Gives details that keep changing, or that do not match their profile.
- Pressures you to keep the relationship secret from your family or friends.
- Becomes angry, sulky or guilt-tripping when you set a boundary.
- Asks for intimate photos.

Never send money to someone you have not met and known for a long time. This is a firm rule, not a judgement on any individual.

## How do you stay safe at a first meeting?

- **Meet in a public place.** A busy café, a park in daylight or a restaurant are all good choices. Avoid anyone's home for early meetings.
- **Tell someone.** Let a friend or family member know who you are meeting, where and when, and agree to message them afterwards.
- **Arrange your own transport.** Get yourself there and back, rather than accepting a lift or giving one.
- **Keep your phone charged.** Know how you will get home.
- **Stay in control.** Keep an eye on your drink and your belongings, and do not feel obliged to move on to a second venue.
- **Trust your instincts.** If something feels wrong, you can leave. A short "Thank you, I need to head off now" is enough. You do not owe anyone an explanation.

Our guide to [your first meeting](/guides/first-meeting) covers where to meet and what to talk about.

## What if you live far apart?

Long-distance introductions are common, especially for people outside the larger Gujarati areas. If you are travelling to meet someone, choose a public, central meeting place, book your own travel and any accommodation yourself, and let someone at home know your plans. Our guide on [finding a match beyond London](/guides/matches-beyond-london) has more on this.

## How can parents help keep things safe?

Parents can play a calm, practical role: being the person their son or daughter tells about a meeting, offering a lift to the station, or being on the end of the phone. It helps to ask rather than interrogate, so your child feels comfortable telling you if something does not feel right. See our [guide for parents](/guides/parents-guide).

## What should you do if something worries you?

If someone makes you uncomfortable, you can stop replying. You are never obliged to continue a conversation or explain why.

If you are concerned about a member's behaviour on the register, email the volunteer team at matrimonial@vanikcouncil.uk so they can look into it.

If you ever feel in immediate danger, call 999. If you have been the victim of fraud, you can report it to Action Fraud.


## Frequently asked questions

### How can I tell if a matrimonial profile is fake?

Watch for details that keep changing or don't match the profile, someone who always avoids video calls or meeting, and anyone who moves very fast or asks for money. A short video call before meeting is the simplest check. On the Vanik Matrimonial Register, every member's identity is checked by a volunteer before they can browse.

### What are common matrimonial scams?

The most common pattern is someone who builds trust quickly, avoids meeting in person, and then asks for money, gifts or bank details to deal with an emergency or a "temporary" problem. Never send money to someone you have not met and known for a long time, however convincing the story.

### Is it safe to give my phone number to a match?

Usually, once you have had a few conversations and feel comfortable. Take your time, and keep your home address, workplace details and financial information private until you know someone well. On the register, contact details are only shared inside the member dashboard when someone requests them.

### Should I video call someone before meeting them?

Yes, it is a good idea. A short video call confirms the person matches their photos and lets you see whether you feel comfortable before you travel to meet them.

### Where do I report matrimonial fraud in the UK?

Report fraud to Action Fraud, and call 999 if you ever feel in immediate danger. If the person is a member of the Vanik Matrimonial Register, also email matrimonial@vanikcouncil.uk so the volunteer team can look into it.
