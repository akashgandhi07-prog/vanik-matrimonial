The most helpful thing a parent can do is support without steering: listen to what your son or daughter wants, help them find good places to meet suitable people, and then trust them to lead. Children who feel trusted tend to involve their parents more, not less.

## Why does it feel harder than it used to?

Many parents met their own spouse through family networks, a word from a relative or an introduction at a samaj event. Those routes still exist, but families are now spread across the country and abroad, people study and work far from home, and many young adults spend their twenties focused on careers.

None of this means your child is not interested in marriage. It often means they have not found the right way to meet people who share their background and values, or they are unsure how to start. That is where you can help.

## How do I start the conversation?

Pick a calm, private moment, not a family gathering or a wedding where aunties are asking questions. Ask rather than tell:

- "Have you been thinking about marriage at all? No pressure, I'd just like to know where you are."
- "What sort of person do you imagine yourself with?"
- "Is there anything I can do to help, or would you rather I stayed out of it for now?"

Then listen. If the answer is "not yet", respect it, and let them know the door is open. A conversation that ends warmly is far more useful than one that ends in an argument.

## What should I avoid saying?

Some well-meant comments land badly:

- Comparisons with cousins or friends' children who are already married.
- Deadlines, such as "you must be settled by 30".
- Comments about weight, looks or career choices framed as "helping".
- Sharing their photo or details with relatives or groups without asking first.
- Treating every introduction as a big family event before the two people have even had a coffee.

Pressure tends to make people withdraw. If your son or daughter is in their twenties, our guide to [finding a partner in your 20s](/guides/finding-a-partner-in-your-20s) might be a useful one to share with them.

## How can I help with their profile?

Offer to be a second pair of eyes, not the author. Useful help includes:

- **Photos.** Offer to take a few clear, natural photos in good light. A relaxed head-and-shoulders photo taken by someone who loves them is usually far better than a selfie.
- **Remembering the good stuff.** Children often undersell themselves. You might remind them of the volunteering they did, the cousins they mentor, or how they cook for the whole ghar at Diwali.
- **Proofreading.** Check spelling and that the details are accurate.

Resist rewriting it in your own words. The person who reads it will be meeting your child, so it should sound like them. Our guide on [writing a matrimonial profile](/guides/writing-your-profile) is a good one to read together.

## What role should I play once introductions start?

Let your son or daughter make the first contact and have the first few conversations on their own. Early meetings are about two people seeing whether they get on, and that is easier without an audience.

Be available. Ask, gently, how it went, and accept "fine" as an answer. If they want your view, give it honestly but kindly. If they decide someone is not right for them, support that decision, even if you liked the family.

When things become more serious, families meeting is a natural and lovely step. Let the couple decide when that happens.

## How do I talk about expectations without imposing them?

It is reasonable for parents to care about shared values, faith and family life. Talk about why these things matter to you, not just what you want. "I'd love you to marry someone who will keep Diwali at home with you, because those memories meant so much to us" invites a conversation. "They must be from our community" closes one down.

Be willing to hear that your child's priorities differ a little from yours. Many young people want the same core things their parents want, such as kindness, stability and family closeness, but describe them differently.

## What about safety?

It is natural to worry when your child meets someone new. Encourage sensible habits: first meetings in public places, telling someone where they are going, and taking things at a comfortable pace. On the Vanik Matrimonial Register every application is checked by a volunteer, including proof of identity, and contact details are only shared inside the member dashboard. Our guide to [staying safe](/guides/staying-safe) covers more.

## What if they are not interested right now?

Then let it be. People come to marriage in their own time, and readiness matters more than timing. Keep the relationship warm, mention options lightly from time to time, and trust that when they are ready they will remember you offered help without pressure.


## Frequently asked questions

### How can I find a suitable match for my son or daughter in the UK?

Start by asking what they want and whether they would like your help. Then point them to places where they can meet people who share your family's background and values, such as a community register, samaj events and trusted family introductions. The Vanik Matrimonial Register is open to adults 18 and over from all Gujarati Hindu and Jain families and is run by Vanik Council volunteers.

### Can parents create a matrimonial profile for their child?

Not on the Vanik Matrimonial Register. The account is created by the person looking to marry, because they are the one who browses, receives introductions and speaks with matches, and volunteers verify their own identity. Parents are very welcome to sit with them while they register and to help with the profile.

### Should I share my son or daughter's biodata in WhatsApp groups?

Only with their agreement. Once a photo and details are shared in a group, you cannot control where they go next, and many young people are upset to find out it has happened. A private register, where contact details are only shared on request, is a safer way for them to be seen.

### What age should my child start looking for a partner?

There is no right age. The register is open from 18, but readiness matters more than timing. Some people want to start in their early twenties, others after training or a few years of work. Ask gently, respect the answer, and leave the door open.

### What if my child wants to marry someone from a different background?

Listen first and ask about the person as an individual. Talk about why your traditions matter to you rather than setting rules, and give the relationship time. Many couples from different backgrounds build happy homes when both families feel heard. For a Hindu and Jain match, our guide to [Hindu and Jain matches](/guides/hindu-jain-matches) may help.

### How do I know a matrimonial service is safe for my child?

Look for real identity checks, privacy for contact details and a clear organisation behind it. On the Vanik Matrimonial Register a volunteer checks every application, including proof of identity, within 2 working days, contact details are only shared inside the member dashboard, and it is run by Vanik Council (UK), registered charity 1137083.
